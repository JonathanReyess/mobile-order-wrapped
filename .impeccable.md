## Design Context

### Users

Duke University students at the end of the semester. The entry point is social — a shared link in a group chat or an Instagram Story. They arrive with a competitive/social mindset: they want to see their numbers, compare them with friends, and immediately have something worth screenshotting. The experience is equal parts meme, flex, and campus inside joke.

**Job to be done**: "Show me something surprising about my eating habits, make it look good enough to put on my Story, and give me something to laugh about with friends."

### Brand Personality

**Tactile · Playful · Collegiate** — or stated another way: *Crisp, Cheeky, Nostalgic.*

- *Tactile*: Interfaces feel physical — subtle paper textures, clean card edges, satisfying micro-interactions
- *Playful*: Light humor, self-aware copy, enough whimsy to match the subject matter (iced lattes and Il Forno)
- *Collegiate*: Rooted in Duke's visual identity — Duke blue is load-bearing, not decorative; it signals "this belongs here"

Emotional arc: **amused shock → pride/shame → urge to share.** The final slide must be screenshot-ready.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Reference**: Arc Browser's website aesthetic — structured card layouts, rich background color with subtle noise/texture overlay, soft pastel accents (cream, pale pink, warm sand), very clean and deliberate typography. Premium web app, not a rave.

**Theme**: Dark-ish but not pure black. Rich Duke blue (#012169 to #00539B range) as the dominant surface, not purple/violet. Texture adds warmth without muddiness.

**Anti-references**:
- Heavy neon gradients (current Spotify Wrapped purple/pink treatment — explicitly leaving this behind)
- Pure black `#000000` dark modes
- Chaotic overlapping text
- Corporate banking dashboard sterility
- Bouncy/elastic animations that feel cheap

**Typography direction**: Look for a typeface that reads like a **well-printed campus publication** or a **high-quality food brand** — somewhere between editorial and approachable. Avoid the reflex fonts. The goal is "someone clearly made a typographic choice here," not "this looks like every other React app."

**Color palette guidance**:
- Dominant: Rich Duke blue family (OKLCH, chroma-controlled, tinted neutrals)
- Secondary: Cream / warm off-white for card surfaces and readable text
- Accent: Soft pale pink or warm sand — used sparingly as a highlight, never as a gradient fill on text
- No pure black, no pure white

### Design Principles

1. **Screenshot-first composition**: Every full-screen slide must look like a finished poster. If it wouldn't look good cropped to a 9:16 Story, fix it.

2. **Texture over emptiness**: Use subtle grain/noise overlays on colored backgrounds. Rich Duke blue with texture reads premium; flat Duke blue reads like a school form.

3. **Typography does the heavy lifting**: Bold hierarchy through size and weight, not color explosions. One memorable typographic moment per slide beats five competing ones.

4. **Surprise earns the share**: Each stat reveal should have a beat of amused recognition — the design should frame the number as the punchline, not bury it.

5. **Motion respects context**: `prefers-reduced-motion` must be honored. Animations enhance; they should never be the only way to understand the content. High text contrast against textured blue backgrounds is non-negotiable.
